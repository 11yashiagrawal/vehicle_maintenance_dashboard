VEHICLE USAGE & AGE
Mileage (km) reflects total usage. High mileage vehicles experience increased wear and require more frequent maintenance.
Vehicle age combined with mileage helps distinguish between gradual aging and aggressive usage.
Engine hours indicate total engine operation. High engine hours with low mileage suggests inefficient usage or excessive idling.
---
THERMAL & LOAD STRESS
Engine load above 80% represents high operational stress.
Oil temperature above 110°C combined with high engine load indicates severe thermal stress and accelerated engine wear.
Thermal stress (temperature × load) is a strong indicator of engine degradation.
---
FAULT & FAILURE SIGNALS
Fault code count represents system errors. High fault counts indicate instability in subsystems.
Fault density (faults per engine hour) provides normalized failure frequency and is a critical predictor of breakdown.
Vehicles with recurring faults require immediate diagnostic inspection.
---
EFFICIENCY & PERFORMANCE
Fuel efficiency (kmpl) reflects combustion quality. Low efficiency under high load suggests internal engine wear or inefficiency.
Engine hours per km is a performance indicator. Higher values indicate poor efficiency and increased mechanical strain.
Load efficiency (engine load / fuel efficiency) reflects stress relative to output.
---
MAINTENANCE HISTORY
Days since last service is a key predictor of failure risk.
Vehicles not serviced for more than 180 days have significantly higher breakdown probability.
Preventive maintenance reduces long-term operational risk.
---
BATTERY & ELECTRICAL SYSTEM
Battery voltage below 11.5V indicates battery degradation and possible failure.
Healthy battery voltage ranges between 12.4V and 12.7V.
---
VIBRATION & MECHANICAL HEALTH
High vibration levels indicate imbalance, misalignment, or worn components.
Persistent vibration increases structural stress and accelerates component failure.
---
ENVIRONMENTAL & OPERATING CONDITIONS
Road condition (urban, rural, highway) affects wear patterns. Rough roads increase mechanical stress.
Weather conditions (hot, cold, humid) impact engine performance and component lifespan.
Hot weather increases overheating risk and thermal stress.
Cold weather affects battery efficiency and startup performance.
---
VEHICLE TYPE & FUEL TYPE
Heavy vehicles (trucks, buses) experience higher load stress and require more frequent maintenance.
Electric vehicles have different failure patterns compared to combustion engines.
Fuel type impacts efficiency, wear, and maintenance cycles.
---
REAL-WORLD MAINTENANCE GUIDELINES
According to general automotive standards, engine oil temperatures above 110°C are considered high and may lead to lubrication breakdown and increased wear.
Preventive maintenance schedules typically recommend servicing vehicles every 3 to 6 months depending on usage intensity.
High engine vibration is a common indicator of mechanical imbalance, misalignment, or worn components.
Battery voltage below 12V indicates potential degradation and risk of failure.
Frequent fault codes are often associated with deeper system issues and should not be ignored.
